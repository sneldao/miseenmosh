# HeyGen Video Contest — Mise en Mosh production brief

Date: 2026-11-26  
Status: **candidate film plan — do not publish or submit without the owner's final selection**

## 1. Purpose

Make a standalone candidate entry for the [HeyGen Video Contest](https://www.heygen.com/blog/video-contest), designed to be good enough to choose after comparing it with the parallel EnigmaGift and Future Selves candidates.

This document is self-contained so an independent production agent can work from this repository alone. It is a production brief, not authorization to post or enter the contest. Paths in backticks are repo-relative unless otherwise stated.

**Owner decision gate:** produce the finished master, provenance table, captions, export, and submission-ready metadata — then stop. The owner decides which of the parallel candidates is actually posted to X and entered in Typeform. Note the rules allow **up to 3 entries per person**: exclusivity between the candidates is a strategy choice (X-feed bandwidth, Most Viral splitting views), not a contest requirement — the owner may choose to enter more than one.

### Non-negotiables

1. **At least 70% of final-video runtime** must be qualifying HeyGen Video 1.0 footage generated through the official **HeyGen MCP** integration, using the entrant's own HeyGen account, during the contest entry period.
2. **No other AI-generated video footage may appear at all.** This includes output from Runway, Pika, Luma, Kling, MiniMax, Hailuo outside the authorized route, or materially regenerated/upscaled output from another video model. Editing, cropping, grading, speed ramps, masks, and ordinary scaling of qualifying HeyGen clips are fine; do not run footage through another AI video enhancer or generator.
3. Up to 30% may be non-qualifying supplementary footage: gameplay screen recordings, title cards, motion graphics, properly licensed still images, original live action, and certain supplementary HeyGen avatar segments.
4. The runtime calculation is based on **screen area over time**. Full-screen gameplay is expensive in this budget. Use it deliberately, mostly near the payoff.
5. The work must be original and unpublished before the contest entry post. **Do not publish the film, public WIP clips, or public teasers before the owner chooses an entry.** The Most Viral clock starts on the contest X post and cannot be restarted.
6. Disclose AI-generated/synthetic content clearly at first exposure in the post or video.
7. Entrant must control rights to all footage, music, sound, images, names, trademarks, scripts, and contributions. No commercial music. No celebrity/public-figure likenesses. No private person's likeness or voice clone without documented consent.
8. No paid promotion or artificial engagement.
9. Keep original generated clips, video IDs, timelines/project files, prompts/versions, and provenance records until at least 90 days after winners are announced.
10. The X post and Typeform are both required only when the owner decides to submit. If submitting, the X copy must begin with `#HeyGenVideoContest` on the first line, tag `@HeyGenMCP`, **and the posting account must follow `@HeyGenMCP`** — the entry only counts with all three.

### What Video 1.0 is

HeyGen Video 1.0 is built on **MiniMax H3** — a general text-to-video model, so cinematic kitchen scenes (not just talking avatars) are in scope. This also explains the otherwise odd rule in §2: Hailuo/MiniMax output made outside the authorized HeyGen MCP route is banned even though it shares the same underlying model. The door qualifies the footage, not the model.

### Practical generation constraint

Qualifying free-plan generations are short clips, historically **15 seconds max at 768p**, and ordinary free allowances are small. The contest materials say contest entrants can receive sufficient free access, but do not assume it is already active.

First production task: generate one harmless test clip through HeyGen MCP and record the returned video ID. If access or monthly allowance blocks production, email `support@heygen.com` with the contest in the subject immediately.

Design for modular 3–10 second shots. Comedy benefits from short clips; do not fight the format with long generations.

### Gameplay-capture caution

Real-time gameplay/screen capture is allowed within the supplementary 30%. However, do **not** include any pre-generated cinematic, video texture, or moving clip inside the game if it was produced by another video-generation model. If any asset's provenance is uncertain, exclude it. Static/generated models, textures, illustrations and voices inside real-time gameplay are a separate rights/documentation question; moving footage generated by another video model is the hard prohibition.

## 3. Differentiation contract

Three candidates are being developed in parallel. They must not converge into interchangeable AI montages.

- **EnigmaGift owns warm tactile mystery:** photographs, a real place, paper, objects, a miniature room, memory clues, seasonal gift emotion.
- **Future Selves owns intimate nocturnal interiority:** one person, one heavy word, voices from possible futures, indigo/gold paper-night cosmology.
- **Mise en Mosh owns loud kinetic comedy:** ticket-storm chaos, kitchen tyranny, the hidden inspector, playable-game energy, `YES, CHEF.`

Do not use another project's signature device. Do not share hero shots, generated clips, music beds, or end-card concepts across the candidates. Mise en Mosh should not become moody epistolary cinema or an emotional gift mystery. It is fast, physical, noisy, funny, and legible in a screenshot.

## 4. Creative strategy

### Working title

**The Inspector Is Already Seated**

### Target category

Primary: **Best Edit**

The film still needs a skit backbone, but its competitive edge should be editorial rhythm: setup → ticket-storm escalation → comic reversals → hard cut into real gameplay → title/button. This distinguishes it from the two emotional story candidates.

If the final cut becomes unusually narrative and the edit is restrained, the owner may choose Best Story or Skit instead. Do not design it as a Product Ad: product proof is the punchline, not the structure.

### One-sentence story

A perfectly ordered kitchen loses control one ticket at a time while the one guest who matters quietly watches — then the film reveals that you can survive service yourself in Mise en Mosh.

### Why this fits the judges

- **Creativity (35%)**: kitchen-command fantasy plus hidden-critic tension plus ticket-storm visual escalation.
- **Craft (25%)**: high-contrast cinematic chaos edited into honest gameplay with sound-led cuts.
- **Storytelling (20%)**: instantly legible comic arc: order → pressure → crack → judgment → playable challenge.
- **Purposeful Video 1.0 showcase (20%)**: generated footage carries the impossible cinematic service; gameplay is the truthful payoff inside the allowed 30%.

### Voice and visual register

Use the game's own design logic:

- authority under pressure
- the pass as a stage
- plate judgment: handle → read → decide → finish → send
- food quality and hospitality as separate evidence
- dining-room consequence
- archetypal brigade members and critics, never real chefs

Visual direction: saturated kitchen color, steel, steam, tile, ticket paper, expressive hands and plates, hard practical light. Avoid making it look like a generic cooking mobile ad. Avoid photoreal close-ups of celebrity-like chefs. Characters are archetypes: Chef, Petra, Dimitri, the Inspector.

## 5. Product-truth boundaries

Mise en Mosh is live on OpenGame: https://opengame.io/g/9i7mjkmdx4

Safe claims:

- playable in the browser
- no install/sign-in required for the basic play path, if still true at capture time
- you judge plates, manage service pressure, and live with the room's response

Rules for honesty:

1. Do not claim multiplayer, mobile-native support, a finished nine-night arc, or full culinary-authorship ambition unless the captured build actually demonstrates it.
2. Do not show a cinematic generated plate interaction as if it is literal gameplay. Use generated footage for the fantasy, then cut clearly to real gameplay.
3. Do not use Gordon Ramsay's name, voice, face, catchphrases, or a deliberate soundalike. The design doc's reference is a tonal reference only; on screen the Chef must be an original archetype.
4. Do not depict real restaurants, critics, chefs, celebrities, or public figures.
5. Kitchen violence must stay comic and non-graphic: smashing plates and throwing a pan into a sink is fine; harming people is not.

## 6. Runtime and footage budget

Target final runtime: **45–60 seconds** — a creative choice, not a constraint; the rules allow anything from 15 seconds to 5 minutes  
Target aspect/master: **16:9, 1080p, 60 fps if practical**; 30 fps is acceptable if capture/render time becomes a risk. Vertical cutdowns are optional and out of scope unless the master is finished early.

Budget target for a 55-second cut:

- **39–42 seconds qualifying HeyGen footage** (roughly low-to-high 70s percent, depending on final runtime)
- **7–9 seconds gameplay capture** (about 13–16%)
- **5–7 seconds titles/end card/share-card graphics** (about 9–13%)

Leave headroom under the 30% cap. The game reveal should feel important even though it is brief.

### Signature moment

The signature moment is the **ticket storm becoming a weather system** while the Chef remains almost still. It expresses the game in one image: authority under pressure.

This is small, ownable, and rewards staying through the title.

## 8. Editing language

The film has one editorial language: **service rhythm**.

- Begin with controlled symmetry and near-silence.
- Use hard cuts and sound-led impacts once tickets multiply.
- Every shot should contain one whole action; enter on the wind-up and cut on the payoff.
- Camera or subject must move in every cinematic shot. No locked-off dead frames.
- Do not use random glitch transitions, default zooms, stock trailer whooshes, or crossfades as glue.
- HUD/UI belongs in the real gameplay section, not faked into generated cinematics.
- The cut into gameplay should feel like the joke landing: cinematic fantasy → real playable mess.
- If music is used, measure its beats before cutting. Kitchen percussion can be the music: printer, pan, plate, ticket tear, brigade calls.

No speed-ramp trick should hide weak action. If slow motion is used, it must be motivated by one payoff, not a default style.

## 9. Text and sound

Minimal text. The film should read silently.

Candidate text spine:

1. `Everything in its place.`
2. `Until the tickets start flying.`
3. `Judge the plate.`
4. `Save the service.`
5. `MISE EN MOSH`

Sound is the comedy engine:

- one ticket dot-matrix print
- printer acceleration
- pan/plate impacts
- spoon/sauce texture
- dining-room glass/pen scratch
- brigade calls, especially controlled `YES, CHEF.` responses
- abrupt silence when the inspector is noticed

Use the game's own owned/licensed voice and sound assets where possible, but document source and rights. Do not use commercial music or a celebrity-chef soundalike.

## 10. Production workflow

Suggested contest workspace:

```text
film/heygen-contest/
  plan/                       # local shot versions, decisions, review notes
  prompts/                    # one markdown/text file per generation batch
  qualifying-clips/           # untouched original HeyGen exports
  gameplay/                   # original gameplay captures + capture notes
  project/                    # edit/HyperFrames/project files
  audio/                      # owned/licensed audio + licence notes
  graphics/                   # end card, titles, share-card elements
  exports/                    # review exports and final master
  provenance.csv              # machine-readable record
  provenance.md               # human-readable summary + runtime math
```

Do not commit large binary exports unless this repo already has a deliberate binary-media workflow. Keep local originals backed up regardless.

If production becomes a full trailer build, use the repo's OpenGame trailer/capture skills rather than improvising: plan the capture, audit what appears on rendered frames, and keep gameplay capture seeded/repeatable where possible. This contest cut is short enough that a lightweight path is acceptable, but not at the cost of fake or unreproducible gameplay.
